This is where a zone's boundary is drawn. The shape you save is the authoritative answer to "which zone is this address in" — when an order's coordinates fall inside it, the order is that zone, full stop. Every other zone's boundary is drawn on the map at the same time so you can trace along the neighbours instead of guessing, because gaps and overlaps between shapes are what send deliveries to the wrong place. You are editing one zone only: the one named in the bar under the title.

What you can do here

Draw New
Clears whatever is there and starts a fresh outline: click to drop each point, double-click to close the shape.
Edit Points
Turns the existing shape's corners into handles you can drag. The small squares on the edges add a new point; select a point and press Delete to remove it.
Clear All
Removes the shape from the map after a confirmation. Nothing is saved until you press Save, so the stored boundary is still intact.
Undo
Steps back through this session's shapes — it remembers the last twenty. Closing the page loses that history.
Layer toggles
Show or hide neighbouring zones and all other zones, for when the reference boundaries get in the way of drawing.
Check Proximity
Tests every point against the other zones' boundaries and flags any that sit within 20 metres, marking them red on the map and listing which zone they are too close to.
Re-fetch QNAS
Pulls the official boundary for this zone from the national address service and loads it onto the map, replacing what is drawn. It asks first, and still needs a Save.
Save Polygon
Writes the shape to the zone and returns you to the zone map. If any points are too close to another zone you get the warning first and can carry on anyway.
Cancel
Leaves without saving, back to the zone map. Everything drawn is discarded.

Reading the map

Current zone
The navy shape — the only one you can edit.
Neighbours
Zones recorded as adjacent to this one. These are the borders your shape should meet cleanly.
Other zones
Everything else that has a boundary, shown for context.
Points
The live corner count of the shape on the map — not what is stored. The info bar above shows the stored count for comparison.
Red circles
Points flagged by the proximity check. Hover one to see which zone it is too close to and by how much.

Worth knowing

  • Saving also moves the zone's centre point to the middle of the new shape, overwriting any point set by hand on the zone map.
  • A boundary needs at least three points, and coordinates are validated on save — a shape that is too small or malformed is rejected rather than half-saved.
  • The proximity warning is advice, not a block. Overriding it is how gaps and overlaps get created, and those are what push an address into the wrong zone.
  • The saved boundary applies to future zone lookups only. Orders already created keep the zone they were given.
  • Clear All, Draw New and Re-fetch QNAS only change the map. Nothing reaches the database until you press Save.
