The scorecard for one day of dispatch. Each row is one rider at one store on that date, written overnight from the batches they completed and the hours they actually worked. Three things are being measured: how much of their work was batched rather than delivered one order at a time, how much of it landed inside the promised delivery time, and how many orders they got through per hour. Click a rider to see the same figures across their last 30 days.

What you can do here

Date
Picks the day being scored. It defaults to today, which is usually still empty — yesterday is the first day with a full set of figures.
Location
Narrows to riders who worked that store. A rider who worked two stores in a day has a separate row for each.
Summary tiles
The four figures at the top are for the rows currently shown: total orders, and the average across riders of batching rate, SLA compliance and orders per hour.
Rider link
Opens that rider's 30-day KPI history.
Sorting
Fixed — busiest rider first by total orders. There is no column sorting or export on this page.

How each number is worked out

Total orders
Orders inside the batches the rider completed that day. Batches still out, or cancelled, count for nothing.
Batched
Orders that travelled in a batch of two or more. Single is the rest — trips where no partner order was found.
Batching %
Batched orders divided by total orders. This is the efficiency number the whole batching engine exists to raise.
SLA %
Compliant orders over compliant plus breached. An order is compliant when its batch was completed on or before that order's own deadline. Green from 90%, amber from 70%, red below.
Orders/hour
Total orders divided by hours worked, where hours come from clock-in to clock-out on completed shifts, minus break time.

Worth knowing

  • These rows are a nightly snapshot, not a live count. Figures for a day appear after the overnight run, so today's date normally shows nothing.
  • A rider is only scored if their shift for that day is Completed. A shift left on Active means the whole day is missing from this page — check the shift before assuming the rider did no work.
  • Hours come from actual clock-in and clock-out. A rider who worked without clocking in has no hours, so orders per hour reads as zero even with orders delivered.
  • A low batching percentage is often a config problem, not a rider problem — a short hold window or thin order flow at that store means there was simply nothing to pair with.
  • The tiles average the rider percentages, so a rider with two orders counts as much as one with forty. Read them as a mood, not a total.
