One rider's dispatch record over the last 30 days. The left column is the summary — totals and averages across the whole period; the table is the day-by-day detail those averages come from, one row per date and store worked. Use it to tell a bad day from a pattern before you act on what the daily KPI dashboard showed. It is a read-only view; nothing about the rider can be changed from here.

What is on the page

Rider info
Driver code, name and the login email the app account uses.
30-day totals
Every order in the batches this rider completed in the period, and how many of those travelled in a batch of two or more.
Averages
The mean of the daily figures, one vote per day — not a recalculation over the whole period. A quiet day counts as much as a busy one.
Daily rows
Newest date first. A rider who worked two stores on one day gets a row for each, because KPIs are kept per store.
Colours
Batching turns green at 50%. SLA is green from 90%, amber from 70% and red below that.

How each number is worked out

Total
Orders in the batches completed that day. Batches still out, or cancelled, count for nothing.
Batched
Orders that shared a trip with at least one other. Single is the remainder — trips where no partner order was found in time.
Batching %
Batched orders over total orders for that day.
SLA %
Orders delivered on time over orders judged. An order is on time when its whole batch finished on or before that order's deadline, which is its creation time plus the store's SLA target.
Orders/hr
Orders divided by hours actually worked — clock-in to clock-out on that day's completed shifts, less break time.

Worth knowing

  • Missing days are not the same as bad days. A date only appears once the nightly run has scored it, and it is only scored if the rider's shift for that day is Completed.
  • Batching percentage is mostly outside the rider's control — it depends on order flow and the hold window set for their store, not on how they drive. Check the store's config before treating a low number as performance.
  • SLA is judged on the batch, so a single slow drop can breach every order on the same trip.
  • A day with orders but zero orders per hour means the rider worked without clocking in, so the system recorded no hours.
  • Nothing here is about pay. Driver earnings and COD are settled on the payout pages, not from these figures.
