The same order book as the main Orders list, but counted up client by client instead of listed one order at a time. Each row is a client, showing how many orders they gave us in the period you pick and how those orders ended up. Use it to see who is busy this week, who has gone quiet, and which client is carrying an unusual number of stuck or cancelled orders. The arrow at the end of a row takes you into the full order list already filtered to that client and date range.

What you can do here

Search
Finds a client by name, code, email, or phone. Press the search button or Enter to apply.
Date buttons
Today, yesterday, last 3 days, last week, or last month — one click sets the range and reloads. Custom reveals two date boxes and applies once both are filled.
Seller pills
A shortcut row of every active client with their lifetime order count. Clicking one jumps straight to that client.
Clear
The cross button drops the search and the dates and puts you back on the full list.
View orders
Opens the Orders page filtered to that client, carrying your date range across so the numbers match what you were looking at.

Reading the columns

Total
Every order that client created inside the date range, whatever became of it.
Pending
Still sitting at the front of the pipe — not yet moved into processing. A high number here on an old date range is worth chasing.
Processing
Accepted and in motion, somewhere between our hands and the customer's.
Done
Delivered. This is the only column that means money earned.
Failed
Cancelled orders. It does not count failed delivery attempts — a task that failed but is still alive stays under Processing.

Worth knowing

  • The five counts follow your date range; the numbers on the pills do not — those are lifetime totals and will not agree with the table.
  • Clicking a pill clears your dates, because it is a plain name search. Set the range again afterwards if you were mid-investigation.
  • Rows are ordered by busiest first, and clients with zero orders in the period are still listed — including ones whose account is no longer active. The pills only show active clients.
  • Search matches the client's stored name exactly as typed, so a pill for a renamed client can come back empty. Search the client code instead.
  • Totals here count orders, not deliveries. One order with several attempts is still one row of the total.
