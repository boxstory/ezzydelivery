Deliveries marked complete a long way from the customer's saved location. One of the two points is wrong and the system cannot tell which: either the driver pressed delivered somewhere other than the drop, or the customer's coordinates are in the wrong place. Nothing is corrected on a guess — staff look at both points and say which is right, and that answer is what moves the record. Widest gaps come first.

The four verdicts

Driver GPS correct
The only one that changes data. The order's coordinates are rewritten to where the driver actually stood, marked as set by the driver, and the road distance is recalculated.
Customer pin correct
The saved address was right and the driver was away from the drop. It records a fact about the delivery; the order is not touched.
Both wrong
Neither point can be trusted — the address needs re-verifying with the customer. Recorded, not corrected.
Not a real discrepancy
A large building, a compound, a loose GPS fix. Closes the row without implying anyone was wrong.

Working the queue

Gap
Straight-line kilometres between the driver's fix and the customer's pin. Only deliveries past the flag threshold appear.
GPS accuracy
How tight the driver's fix was, in metres. A loose fix weakens the evidence — read the gap against it before deciding.
Driver filter
Only drivers who actually have flagged deliveries, with their counts. A driver appearing repeatedly is the pattern worth chasing.
Bulk decisions
Tick several rows and give one verdict, up to 200 at a time. Rows already reviewed are skipped rather than overwritten.
Notes
Saved against every row in the batch, along with your name and the time.

Good to know

  • A verdict is final for that row — it leaves the pending queue and is not re-decided by a later batch.
  • Bulk-accepting driver GPS moves real addresses. It is the one verdict worth deciding row by row.
