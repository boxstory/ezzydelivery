Qatar is addressed by zone numbers, and this is the master list of them. Every row is one zone: its number, its English and Arabic names, whether we have drawn a boundary for it, how many named areas sit inside it, how many zones it borders, and which groups it belongs to. This table is reference and repair work — the zone a delivery lands in is worked out from these records, so a gap here shows up later as a driver who cannot find the address.

What you can do here

Search
Matches the zone number, the English or Arabic zone name, and the names of the areas inside it — so typing a neighbourhood finds its parent zone. If nothing matches exactly it retries with fuzzy matching, which forgives most typos and spellings.
Group filter
Narrows to the zones in one group. Only active groups are listed.
Polygon filter
Shows only zones that have a boundary drawn, or only those that do not — the fastest way to work through the gaps.
Edit polygon
The polygon icon at the end of a row opens the boundary editor for that zone, where you draw or correct its outline on a map.
View Map
Opens the same zones on an interactive map — better for judging whether two boundaries actually meet.

What the columns mean

Zone
Qatar's official zone number. It is the identifier everything else hangs off, and it is unique.
Polygon
The zone's drawn boundary and how many points it has, or None. A boundary needs at least three points to count.
Areas
How many named neighbourhoods sit inside the zone. These are what customers actually write in an address.
Neighbours
How many zones border this one. The relationship works both ways — set it on either side and both are linked.
Groups
The wider delivery areas the zone belongs to. Drivers choose groups, not individual zones, to say where they want to work.

Worth knowing

  • Zone detection is polygon first: a coordinate is matched to the zone whose boundary contains it. Only when no boundary contains it does the system fall back to the nearest zone centre — which is a guess, and the usual reason an order lands in the wrong zone.
  • So the Without Polygon count is the real backlog on this page. Drawing those boundaries is what makes zone assignment accurate.
  • Saving a polygon also moves the zone's centre point to the middle of the new shape.
  • Only active zones take part in zone detection, even though inactive ones are still listed here.
  • The three counters at the top cover every zone in the system, not just what your filters are showing.
