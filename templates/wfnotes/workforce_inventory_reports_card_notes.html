A read-only picture of what stock our sellers are holding. It opens on one card per active client that has products on file; clicking a card drills into that client's full product list with the quantity we currently hold for each item. Nothing on this page changes stock — it is a reporting view over the inventory records that warehouse and product staff maintain. When you want the shortlist of items that actually need ordering, use Restock List instead.

What you can do here

Seller filter
Types-as-you-go and hides any seller card that does not match the text. It filters the cards on the opening screen only — it does not search inside a seller's product table once you have drilled in.
Open a seller
Click anywhere on the card to load that client's products: name, SKU, category, stock on hand, reorder level, status and the date the stock figure was last touched.
Back to all
Back to All Sellers returns to the card grid and clears the selected seller.
Total products
On each card, how many distinct products that client has on file — not how many units are in stock.

Stock status

In stock
Quantity on hand is above the reorder level. Nothing to do.
Low stock
Quantity has fallen to the reorder level or below but is not zero. These are the items that show up on the Restock List.
Out of stock
Quantity is zero — either genuinely empty, or the product has no inventory record at all, which reads the same way here.

Worth knowing

  • Stock is the sum of every inventory row for that product, across locations. There is no per-warehouse breakdown on this page.
  • The reorder level is a single fixed threshold applied to every product — it is not a per-product setting you can tune from here.
  • Only clients that are active and have at least one product appear in the card grid. A client with no products is simply absent, not shown as empty.
  • Last Settled on the seller cards is not fed by this report and will read Never.
  • This page is read-only. Corrections to quantities happen in the product and warehouse screens, not here.
