This is the invoice for one COD payout to a business — the document that proves how much customer cash was released, what EzzyDelivery kept back, and what was actually transferred. It is created automatically when a payout is handed over on the Client Payout desk, and you land straight on it afterwards. The same page is shown to the business itself from their COD statement, so treat it as the seller's copy: it is the only account of the payout they see.

What you can do here

Print invoice
Prints or saves just the sheet — the toolbar and these notes are left out of the printed page.
Back
Returns to Payout History, where every payout ever made is listed and can be reopened.
Invoice code
The reference for this payout. Copy it into Void a payout on the Client Payout desk if the money has to be clawed back.

Reading the sheet

Order rows
Every delivery attached to this payout, with the day it was delivered, the delivery charge billed for it and the COD collected from the customer.
Total COD
Gross customer cash across those rows, before anything comes off.
Deduction lines
Each thing withheld, on its own line — delivery charges plus any fulfilment, cargo/handling or other charge staff added at payout time.
Net paid
What was transferred to the business. This is the figure the bank transfer, Fawran payment or cash handover must match.
Void stamp
A voided payout keeps its invoice and carries the void date, code and reason across the face of it.

Worth knowing

  • Nothing on this page can be edited — an invoice is a record of money that already moved. To undo it, void the payout on the Client Payout desk.
  • The delivery charge on each row is the figure frozen at payout time. Editing a charge afterwards in Client Charges will not change what this invoice says.
  • Voiding does not delete or reissue the invoice; it adds the stamp and returns those deliveries to the payout desk as unpaid.
  • Older invoices made before the freeze existed fall back to the live charge, so a very old invoice can restate if its charge was edited since.
  • The business sees this same document, so a wrong reference or payment method here is a question you will be asked about.
