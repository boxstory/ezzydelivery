Describes how the building is divided inside. Zones hold racks, racks hold shelves, shelves hold bins — and a bin is the smallest place a product can sit. You set the counts and the naming style here; saving only records the plan, and the next screen shows what it will produce before anything is created.

The four numbers

Zones
The big divisions of the floor. At least one.
Racks per zone
Applied to every zone equally — this is a grid, not a per-zone layout.
Shelves per rack
Levels on a rack.
Bins per shelf
The actual storage slots. Zones × racks × shelves × bins is the total the system will create.

Naming

Zone pattern
A comma-separated list such as A,B,C,D — those become the zone codes.
Rack, shelf, bin
Numeric or lettered. Codes are built by joining the levels, so a bin ends up as something like A-1-2-3.

Good to know

  • Match the real building. These codes are what pickers read on the shelf — invented structure makes the route sheet useless.
  • Nothing is created yet. Saving records the plan; the preview screen is where you generate the locations.
  • Be careful re-configuring a live warehouse — regenerating can offer to delete existing locations, and stock sits in those.
  • Bin count grows fast: four numbers multiply, so 4 × 10 × 5 × 10 is two thousand bins.
