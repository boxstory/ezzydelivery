The product catalogue for one business, opened from that client's licence record. The form on the left adds an item to their catalogue; the list on the right is everything they already have, newest first. Products matter because orders and warehouse stock are recorded against them — an order line that names an item we do not hold cannot be picked or counted. Use this when a client sends a product list and someone has to key it in for them.

What you can do here

Brand / Item
Both required. Together they are how the product reads everywhere else in the system, so write them the way the client does.
SKU
Required. The client's own code for the item — this is what imported orders and stock counts are matched on.
Barcode
Optional. Fill it in if the item is scanned in the warehouse; leave it blank rather than inventing one.
Price
Required, in QAR. This is the client's selling price, not a delivery charge.
Initial quantity
Optional opening stock. Leave it at zero if nothing has been received yet — a stock record is only created when this is above zero.
Add product
Saves the item to this client's catalogue and reloads the page with it in the list. Reset just clears the form.
Search
Filters the list already on screen as you type — it matches anything in the row, not just the name.
Back link
Returns to this client's licence and document record.

Reading the list

Product ID
The system's own reference, generated on save. Not the client's SKU.
Stock
Total quantity across every inventory record for that item. It is highlighted once it drops below ten.
Order
Newest product first, so whatever you just added is at the top of the list.

Worth knowing

  • Everything added here belongs to this business only — the catalogue is per client, and the same physical item for two clients is two separate products.
  • The SKU is what imported orders match on. A typo here is not visible until orders start failing to find their product, so check it before saving.
  • Nothing stops you adding the same SKU twice. Search the list before you add.
  • The stock figure is the sum of the item's inventory records; it is not adjusted from this page beyond the opening quantity.
  • Despite the address of this page, it manages products — the naming is historical.
