Qatar's delivery zones drawn on a real map. Each numbered pin is one zone's centre point; switching on polygons shows the actual boundaries we hold for them. Use it to see how the network fits together — where boundaries are missing, where two zones overlap or leave a gap, and which zones we have marked as neighbours. The table underneath lists the same zones and is where the per-zone actions live.

What you can do here

Layer buttons
Markers shows the zone pins, Polygons the drawn boundaries, Neighbours the lines between zones we have marked as adjacent, and Labels the zone names on the map. Each one toggles on and off.
Group pills
Show only the zones in one group. Markers, labels and polygons all follow the choice; All Zones puts everything back.
Zone popup
Clicking a pin gives the zone's name, coordinates, groups, polygon point count and its neighbours, plus buttons to edit the point or the boundary.
Focus
The crosshair in the table zooms the map to that zone, highlights it green, dims the rest and marks its neighbours in amber.
Edit point
Opens the toolbar over the map: drag the marker to the new centre — the coordinates update live — then Save Point, or Cancel to leave it alone. The table row updates in place.
Edit polygon
Opens the full boundary editor for that zone on its own page.
Reset View
Back to the whole-country view, clearing highlights and neighbour lines. It does not undo anything you saved.

Reading the map

Navy pin
An ordinary zone marker, sitting on that zone's centre point.
Green
The zone you have focused on.
Amber
A neighbour of the focused zone, and the lines drawn between adjacent zones.
Boundary colour
A polygon is tinted by the zone's first group, so groups read as coloured regions rather than a wall of navy.
Total / With / Missing
All zones on record, those with usable coordinates, and those without. Only the middle group can be drawn at all.

Worth knowing

  • Zones counted under Missing are not on this map or in the table beneath it — a zone you cannot find here probably has no coordinates yet.
  • Moving a centre point saves the moment you press Save Point. There is no undo and no confirmation.
  • A centre point is a fallback, not a boundary. Deliveries are matched to a zone by polygon first; only when no boundary contains the coordinate does the nearest centre decide it — which is exactly when a wrong pin sends an order to the wrong zone.
  • Saving a boundary in the polygon editor also moves that zone's centre point to the middle of the new shape, overwriting anything you set here.
  • Neighbour links are symmetrical — recorded once, they apply to both zones.
