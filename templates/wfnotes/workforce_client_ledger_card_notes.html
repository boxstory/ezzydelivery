This is one client's account. Every other finance screen shows a slice of a client's money — COD on one page, invoices on another — and none of them add up to a figure. This one does. Each money event is a single line, and the Balance column is the answer to "where does this account stand right now".

How to read it

Balance
Always means what EzzyDelivery owes the client. A negative balance means the client owes us instead — the headline says which in words, so you never have to read a minus sign to know the direction.
Credit
Raises what we owe. COD we collected for the client is a credit — we are holding their money. So is a payment they make against a charge.
Debit
Lowers what we owe. A delivery charge, an advance paid out early, and the COD payout itself are all debits — the payout discharges the liability, which is why a fully settled account reads zero.
Filters do not move the balance
Narrowing to one segment hides rows but never restates the Balance column — it stays the account's real balance at each line. A running total of only the rows you filtered to would look like a statement and be wrong.

Things worth knowing

Opening balance
An account that has never been opened starts at zero even though the client may already hold COD and owe invoices. Post opening balance seeds it with what is already documented — COD we hold, less unpaid invoices. Charges not yet on an invoice are deliberately left out, or they would be billed twice.
Nothing is ever deleted
A reversed entry stays on the account, struck through and marked Void, with a matching contra entry beside it. The two cancel. That is why the ledger can be trusted as a record rather than a view.
Status
Pending is money that exists but has not reached us — COD still in a driver's pocket. Open is realised and awaiting settlement. Cleared is done.
