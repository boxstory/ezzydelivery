Some clients contract for proof of delivery — evidence that a parcel really reached the customer, or really could not be handed over. This page is where that requirement is switched on, one client at a time. With it on, the driver app will not let the driver close the task until they have captured what the client asked for, and neither will the API: the rule is checked on the server, so it cannot be skipped by a driver on a patchy connection or by a client's own integration posting the status directly.

The same row also decides whether the client gets live tracking — a map in their own dashboard following their driver from the moment the ride starts. Both are per-client switches: turn them on for the clients who asked, not across the board.

What you can do here

Proof on delivered
The driver must capture proof before Delivered or Partial will go through. Partial counts because goods changed hands — otherwise it would be the easy way around the rule.
Proof on failed
The driver must take a photo before Failed will go through — the closed door, the wrong building, the refused parcel still in their hand. This is the one clients ask for when they are arguing about attempts.
Counts as proof
What a successful delivery needs: a photo, the customer's signature drawn on the driver's screen, or both. The box greys out when Proof on delivered is off, because it only governs that outcome.
Live tracking
Puts a Live Tracking link in this client's own sidebar, showing their driver's position on a map. The link carries a count that lights up the moment a driver taps Start Ride, and the map only follows deliveries that are actually on their way — a parcel still sitting at the hub is not shown.
Driver sees
A plain-English read-back of the rule as the driver will meet it, updated as you change the row. Check this line before saving.
Save
Saves that one row only, so nothing else on the page can change by accident. Every change is written to the log with your username.

Where the proof ends up

On the order
Photos and signatures appear on the order details page under Proof of Delivery, where the client can see them too.
On the task
Staff see the same files on the delivery task detail page, alongside the completion GPS point the app records with them.
Who can open them
Proof files are served through Django, not straight off disk — only the owning client and staff can open one. A link is not a public link.

What the client's map shows

Deliberately thin
A pin for the drop-off, a live dot for the driver, the task number and its status. It is not the staff task view — no customer phone number, no order file, no driver identity beyond "driver on the way".
Only their own
Scoped to the client's own deliveries. A client never sees another client's parcels or a driver who is not carrying theirs.
When the dot stops
The driver's position carries its own age ("4 min ago"), and once the app has been quiet for ten minutes the dot greys out and says so — a parked driver and a closed app look different, which saves you the phone call.

Worth knowing

  • A failed attempt always takes a photo, whatever the proof type says. There is no customer standing there to sign for a parcel they did not take, so a signature requirement would leave the driver stuck on the doorstep.
  • The rule is read when the driver submits, not when the order was created — turning it on binds every task already out on the road.
  • Proof the driver already uploaded counts. A submit that failed on a dead connection will not be blocked a second time by the photo its own first attempt managed to send.
  • Turning this on makes the driver's day slower. Every closed task costs a camera step, and a driver with no signal has to wait for the upload — switch it on for clients who asked, not across the board.
  • Staff closing a task from the office are not stopped by the proof rule. It binds the driver app and the API; a staff correction on the task page is a deliberate override.
  • Live tracking shows only what the driver's phone has actually reported. A browser cannot take GPS fixes once the app is closed, so gaps are real gaps — the map says so rather than guessing a position.
  • Every client trading when this setting was introduced already had the tracking page, so they were switched on to avoid taking a working feature away. Turn one off here if a client should not have it.
