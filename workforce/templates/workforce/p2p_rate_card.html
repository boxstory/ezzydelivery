Vehicle capacity

What each vehicle takes, in cubic metres. A motorcycle box is a 30 cm cube — 0.027 cbm, which is one small box and not three. Both ends are enforced: a load bigger than the vehicle holds is not priced on it, and a load under “send out from” is not either — a pickup set to 1.0 is not offered for a parcel a car could take. The minimum is ignored when nothing smaller can carry the load, so it can never strand a job between two vehicles. The chart names the vehicle to use instead. Switch a row off to drop both rules.

Box limits

The most boxes of one size a vehicle takes, whatever the cubic metres say. Volume is only half of what stops a load: a car holds 0.4 cbm, which is nine small boxes on paper and six in the boot. Three medium boxes and six small ones cannot both be written as a cbm ceiling — 0.141 against 0.090 — so the count lives here instead. Leave a count blank, switch a row off, or add no row at all and that pairing has no count rule: the cbm ceiling above decides on its own, exactly as it did before this table existed. Over the count reads as “too big” and the chart names the vehicle to use instead.

Box tiers

What carrying more than one box adds, on top of whichever band the job resolves to. Deliberately not part of a band: a per-tier row for every combination would turn a {{ band_count }}-row card into a {{ band_count }}×{{ box_tiers|length }} one.

A row can be bounded by an absolute box count, by how full the vehicle is, or by both — and every bound it states has to hold. Full from / to is a fraction: 0.5 is half, 1 is full, blank means the row is not about fullness. That is what makes one slab mean the same thing in every vehicle — a car that takes six medium boxes reads “up to 0.5” as 1–3 boxes and a van reads the identical row as 1–10. Leave both blank for a rule that is about the job rather than the load, the way “past eleven boxes a human prices it” is true in a car and in a van alike. The narrowest covering row wins, counting both bounds; a row that needs a quote beats every other.
