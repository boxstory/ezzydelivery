## 18:40 — Waybill code switched from Code128 barcode to QR
Drivers could not scan the label: the Code128 was stretched across the label width with `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, which put each module at roughly 0.22–0.30mm — about two dots on a 203dpi thermal head, and far under what a phone camera resolves. Replaced it with a QR (`generate_qr_svg` / `generate_qr_image` in label_utils), same payload as before (`order.order_number`), error correction M, emitted as one rect per run of dark modules in a square viewBox with crispEdges so the printer rasterises at its own DPI. Modules are now 0.96mm at 24mm (0.80mm at 20mm on a 58mm roll) — 3-4x bigger, and QR is orientation-free with error correction on top. All three waybill views (business, staff, bulk) and the stored PNG label now render the QR; every driver and staff scanner already listed `qr_code` in its BarcodeDetector formats, so no app change was needed.

Also fixed a pre-existing cascade bug in the same file: the `@container` blocks sit ABOVE the footer base rules, so the narrow-roll overrides for the code and its number never applied at equal specificity. The QR/number responsive rules now sit after the base declarations — verified 24mm on 80mm/A6/4x6 and 20mm on the 58mm roll.

## 21:24 — P2P return trip: the leg back, as a second order

A point-to-point job could only ever go one way, so anything that has to come back — signed papers, an empty crate, an item after a repair — could not be booked at all. Added a **return trip** option to every P2P form: the two cards on the public calculator (step 6), the customer booking form, the customer edit page and the staff booking form. Ticking it writes a SECOND order running the other way — collected at the delivery address, delivered to the pickup one — rather than a flag on the first, because a driver has to be dispatched to it, someone has to receive it, and it has to be billed, and the Order already has all of that machinery. Both legs sit at `to_review` with a `pending` pickup location until the customer confirms, and confirming releases the pair together.

Pricing is the rate card charged twice: the leg back is the same journey reversed carrying the same load, so `quote()` gained a `return_trip` flag that doubles the resolved band, returning `leg_price` beside the total. The agreed figure splits across the two orders (`return_fee` is half, `outbound_fee` the remainder) so the job is never billed twice, and a staff price that overrules the card splits the same way. COD stays on the outward leg only — asking the receiver twice is the one mistake here that costs money. The five near-identical result dicts inside `quote()` were folded into one local `result()` helper so a new key cannot be added to four of them.

Also, per the follow-up ask: **"Number of boxes" now totals itself from the per-size counters** on all three booking forms — the server already rewrote it in `clean()` and the public calculator's stepper already followed the size cards, this just makes it visible before the POST (readonly while a mix is entered, handed back with the customer's own number when the counters are cleared). And the calculator's per-size counts now actually reach the booking form: `fillBookingForm` wrote `load_<size>` into hidden inputs that were never in the markup, so every mixed load silently arrived as a single size. Verified in a headless browser on production (40 → 80 QR on the toggle, 2 S + 3 M → 5 boxes) plus 23 new tests in `p2p/tests_return.py` and 3 on the staff side; 227 passing across `p2p`, the staff P2P suites and message templates.

## 21:38 — One place says how many boxes, and it has a stepper

Both the calculator and the booking form carried a standalone box-count control next to the per-size counters, and one field can only hold one number — so a job counted on three cards left it stating a fourth. Five small plus a medium plus a large read as "2 boxes" in the stepper while the line underneath read "7". Removed the outside total on all four surfaces and gave the counters themselves &minus;/+ steppers:

- **Public calculator**: the "How many boxes?" stepper is gone (with `setBoxes` / `renderBoxStepper` and 76 lines of dead CSS). The cards already had their own steppers; what is left is those plus a running total.

Two things had to follow. The counters are now the only place a count is entered, so they are **prefilled** from the booking (`load_initial`) — from its lines when it has them, and from the scalar size/box_count pair when it does not, or re-saving a three-box job from the edit page would have quietly returned one. ## 21:55 — XL left every vehicle selectable, including the motorcycle

Reported from the calculator: pick **Bulky item** and all five vehicle cards stay lit, so the page offers a motorcycle for a 25 kg+ item. Reproduced in a browser — S and L grey correctly, XL greys nothing.

The cause was the shape of the answer, not the rule. `vehicleShouldBe` returned *the vehicle to use instead*, with `null` meaning "this one is right". A bulky item has no vehicle the card prices at all (`P2P_VEHICLE_FITS['xl']` is empty, so `smallest_vehicle_for` is None), so the too-big branch had no alternative to name and returned `null` — indistinguishable from a clean bill. The server was refusing the bike the whole time; the page just could not tell. Rewrote it as `vehicleVerdict` returning `{ok, reason, needed}`, the same three parts `p2p.pricing.vehicle_verdict` returns, and updated both call sites.

Now the bike greys for a bulky item and the note reads "We match a vehicle to 1 × bulky / extra large by hand — pick the one you think it needs, or leave it to us, and we'll confirm the price." Car through truck stay selectable deliberately: a van really can take a bulky item, the server accepts it, and all 13 `xl` bands are `needs_quote`, so the result panel already routes the job to a human. Greying all five would have been a dead end and would have made the page disagree with the server. Three tests added in `p2p/tests.py` pinning what the page mirrors — nothing carries an xl automatically, a bike is refused one with no alternative named, and every xl quote goes to a human whatever the vehicle. 209 passing.

## 22:18 — The box total says what the load measures

The running total read "9 boxes" and stopped there, which says nothing about whether it will go in a car. It now reads **"9 boxes · 0.423 cbm"** — the volume the load actually takes up, on the calculator's size step and on all three booking forms.

The figure comes off the same per-size table the fit rules use (`P2P_SIZE_CARDS`), so what the customer reads is exactly what decides which vehicle cards grey out below it: a small box is 0.015 cbm, a bike's box is 0.027, and nine small boxes at 0.135 are an SUV's job. The calculator already had the numbers in its injected capacity payload; the booking forms get each size's figure on the stepper itself (`data-size-cbm` off `form.load_rows`) rather than needing a second payload injected into three pages.

Checked against the server for four loads — 9 × S, 1 × XS, the 5+1+1 mix and 9 × M — and the page and `p2p.pricing.load_cbm_of` agree to the millilitre on all four (0.135, 0.004, 0.230, 0.423).
