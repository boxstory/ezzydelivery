# Dev Log — 2026-09-03

## 07:04 — External navigation handoff: explain (and reconstruct) the Waze trail gap
A driver tapping Waze or Google Maps backgrounds the PWA, and a backgrounded browser cannot take a
GPS fix at all — so the trail went dark for the whole drive and looked identical to a dead phone.
Added a `DriverNavHandoff` record that opens when the driver leaves for a nav app and closes when
they come back, closed by either the PWA's return event or the next live ping (whichever lands
first, idempotently). The live map now shows those drivers in violet as "In Waze since 14:32 —
position frozen" with a dashed projected leg to the destination instead of a stale grey marker, the
GPS-heartbeat alert no longer fires for an explained silence, and the missing leg is reconstructed
from the self-hosted OSRM route (stored as an estimate, never written back as DriverLocation rows).
Battery cost is one request out plus one fix and one request back per trip to the nav app.

## 07:14 — Handoff detection moved from the button to the link
The first pass only covered the buttons I had wired by hand, but drivers leave the PWA from many
more: Navigate, Maps, Pin, the pickup and hub tiles, Call and WhatsApp, spread across ten templates.
The click listener in `pwa_base.html` now decides from the URL itself — waze.com, google maps,
wa.me/whatsapp, `tel:`, `geo:`, or any absolute link off our own origin — and reads the destination
coordinates straight out of the link, so new buttons are covered without being registered anywhere.
Providers gained `whatsapp` and `phone`, and the model now owns the phrase the map shows ("In Waze",
"On a call") instead of the client stitching one together from a provider name. The three WhatsApp
buttons that open from script call `EzzyGPS.navHandoff.openUrl()` directly, since no anchor is clicked.

## 07:31 — Verified the in-nav status on the real live map, and fixed what it claimed
Checked the handoff display against production rather than tests: driver 686 had already recorded two

## 07:37 — Why the 0-second handoff, and the holes no button can see
Investigated the 1-second handoff on driver 686. Its fix stream has no hole at all around it
(07:16:23 → 07:16:31), so the tap filed a handoff and the app was back a second later — the record was
harmless but told us nothing. The same trail, though, has a 127s hole and a 651s hole with **no**
handoff at all: those started with a locked screen or the home button, which no click listener can
ever see, and they are most of what actually stops the trail.

So the handoff no longer depends on a button. `visibilitychange → hidden` now files a `background`
handoff when no link was tapped ("App in background"), and confirms `left_foreground` on the link
handoff when one was. Spells under 45s are discarded server-side on close, so a glance at a
notification leaves nothing behind. Added `close_reason` (return / ping / superseded) and
`left_foreground` so the next odd handoff diagnoses itself instead of needing this dig. The GPS-lost
alert deliberately still fires for background spells — a phone locked in a pocket for ten minutes
mid-delivery is exactly what that alert is for — and is only excused for a deliberate tap.

## 08:46 — Driver timeline: one driver's day drawn on a map
New staff page at `/workforce/drivers/<id>/timeline/`, linked from the driver profile. Leaflet map
with the measured trail solid in maroon, every missing stretch dashed (violet where a handoff explains
it, grey where nothing does), stop and status-change pins, and a chronological list beside it that
pans the map when a row is clicked. Header carries distance, moving, stopped and dark minutes.

Gap detection is scaled off the day's own median reporting interval rather than a fixed threshold —
the duty cycle is 30s on a task and 3 min idle, so any fixed number either cries wolf at an idle
driver or misses a real absence on a busy one. Where a nav handoff covers a hole its own times and
reason win, since it knows exactly when the driver left and which app took them; the rest are labelled
Unexplained rather than guessed at. Positions are pruned after 7 days, and the page says so for older
days instead of drawing an empty map.

Verified against production: driver 686's day came back with `08:23–08:24 On a call (0.8 km)` and
`08:29–08:42 App in background (10.1 km reconstructed)` — the second is exactly the class of hole that
was invisible before this morning's background detection.
