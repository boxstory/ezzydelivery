## 08:13 — Replacement orders Phase A: refund ceiling and routing
Added `orders/money.py`, the first piece of the replacement/exchange feature. It answers two
questions nothing in the codebase could answer before: how much may still be refunded on an
order, and which leg can actually pay it (the driver's wallet, the seller's ledger float, or
neither). Refund amounts are typed by staff — item prices are not usable as a source, since 28%
of orders carry no OrderItem rows and 78% of the rows that exist have no unit_price.

Also fixed a real ceiling bug: `_collected_cod_for` in business/views.py counted the gross
collection, so two sequential returns on one order each believed the whole amount was still
refundable. It now delegates to `money.collected_for`, which is net of refunds already made.

15 tests added in `orders/tests_money.py`, all passing. Server reloaded, site HTTP 200.

## 08:36 — Replacement orders Phase B: the order, the link and the staff button
Staff can now send a replacement from an order's detail page. A new `replaces` self-FK on Order
makes the link queryable (the old Repeat Order button recorded it as a sentence in a comment),
alongside `replacement_reason` and `collect_back` for exchanges where the driver takes the old
goods away on the same visit. Migration 0069 is purely additive.

`orders/services.py` is the single code path — staff, and later the client, both go through it.
Settlement is typed by staff in the modal: even exchange, collect extra, or refund. A collect
becomes ordinary COD on the new order; a refund is reported with the leg that can actually pay it
rather than being silently written as a negative COD.

Two traps handled deliberately. A pick & drop replacement keeps its `dl_amount`, because driver
pay there is a percentage of it — zeroing the fee would have paid the driver nothing; the client
side is zeroed with `verified_delivery_charge=0` instead. And publishing goes through
`apply_ready_and_publish`, not the task builder, so stock is reserved and the first-mile leg opens
rather than sending a driver for goods nobody set aside. Both have regression tests.

Caught in review: the modal was first placed between `{% endblock %}` and `{% block extra_js %}`,

## 09:05 — Replacement orders Phase C: refunds that actually move money
The refund half now executes instead of just reporting. `orders/services.issue_refund` routes the
money to whichever leg can pay it: while the driver still holds the cash it comes out of his COD,
and once that money has been settled to the seller it comes off their ledger float instead. Picking
the wrong one would either leave a driver short at hand-in or refund a customer with money we never
received, so the routing is the whole point rather than a convenience.

Three things were unblocked or fixed along the way:

- **More than one refund per task.** `record_cod_return` rejected any second return outright, so a
  customer handing goods back over two visits was impossible. The cap is now the running total read
  inside the driver lock — still never more than was collected, but no longer one-shot.
- **Partial refunds no longer report as complete.** Refunding 100 against a 300 reversal used to
  flip every open ReturnRequest to a green "Processed" badge. Returns are now only closed once the
  refunded total actually covers them.
- **The seller-facing COD status no longer goes stale.** A full refund wrote `cod_status_by_staff`
  directly and left the client reading "Collected" after their customer had the money back. It now
  goes through `apply_cod_status` so both ladders move together.

Also wired `ledger_service.post_refund`, which finally gives `available_refund_credit` a caller —
it was fully written, documented and orphaned. It refuses above the float, reading that figure
inside the transaction so two simultaneous refunds cannot both spend it.

`ReturnRequest.replacement_order` (migration 0070) links a return to the goods sent to replace it;
until now nothing connected the two workflows.

76 new tests pass. Full fleet suite 338 green — no regression from the WalletService change.

## 09:28 — Replacement orders Phase D: the exchange visit
An exchange is one trip that goes both ways — the driver hands over the replacement and brings the
original back. That had no representation: `task_leg` only knew `single` and `hub_delivery`, so an
exchange would have been priced and shown as an ordinary drop.

Added the `exchange` leg, and gave it its own line on the rate card rather than letting it fall
through to the flat normal fee. It starts level with a normal delivery, so no driver's pay changes
on the day this ships, but staff can now raise it for the round trip instead of the figure being an
accident of branch ordering. The branch sits ahead of the order-type ones deliberately: pricing an
exchange as a pick & drop percentage would pay it off a delivery charge the replacement does not
carry — i.e. nothing.

Both earnings resolvers were updated together. `driver_fee()` and `fee_expr()` are twins, and a
branch added to one and not the other would make the payout queue price a delivery differently from
the task page showing it; there is now a test asserting the two agree across every leg.

Drivers get an amber "collect the old item" banner above the address on an exchange task — above,
not in the figures strip at the bottom, because leaving without the original wastes the whole trip.

Caught in review: the banner test first failed silently for two unrelated fixture reasons (the view
is gated on `Profile.is_driver`, and the task list requires `dl_task_publish=True`). Worth knowing
for any future driver-PWA test.

Not built: driver-initiated refunds at the door. Refunds remain staff-recorded — see below.

88 new tests pass. Full fleet suite 338 green, delivery 163 green.

## 07:13 — WA inbox: media was never loading (two real bugs)
Chased "still not loading" by driving the real page in headless Chromium against a local mirror of the nginx /waha split, which surfaced two separate faults. First, WAHA builds its file URLs from its own container hostname and hands back "http://localhost:3000/api/files/...", a host the rewriter did not know and the browser cannot reach, so CSP refused every photo, voice note and document; the rewriter now also strips localhost and, as a catch-all, any host at all in front of an /api/files/ path. Stored messages now point at a new streamer at /waha/wa-chats/media/<id>/ which reuses the CRM's hardened streamer (archive first, live WAHA fallback, mimetype clamped).

The streamer reuses the CRM platform-account read gate, but our own inbox number is on both sides of every message in this inbox and is itself a registered profile, so passing both parties refused 45 of the 300 newest media rows. The gate now reads the counterparty only, which is who the rule is about; that leaves 24 of 300 still refused, all of them conversations with a registered driver or client. Their message text is already displayed by the messages API beside this endpoint, so those show as broken attachments — flagged to the user for a decision rather than removed.

## 10:20 — Replacement orders Phase E: client-facing, and the return permission hole
Sellers can now request a replacement from their own order page. The client path deliberately
cannot publish and cannot set a collect amount — a replacement raised here lands as a draft for
staff to confirm, and money at the door stays an ops decision. What the seller controls is the
request, the reason, and whether the driver should bring the original back.

New `ORDER_REPLACE` permission, granted to Owner/Manager/Staff and withheld from Viewer, wired
into ALL_PERMISSIONS and PERMISSION_GROUPS so it is assignable in the team UI.

The important half was closing the return hole: `return_update_status` carried no permission
check at all, so a read-only Viewer could approve a return — which writes back to OrderItem
quantities — or mark it refunded. It now requires ORDER_REPLACE.

I first gated approved/rejected/refunded to staff, then corrected that: there is no staff-side
return console anywhere in the codebase, so restricting approval to staff would have meant nobody
could ever approve a return. The rule now is narrower and matches who owns the decision — a seller
may approve or reject their own return, because that is their goods and their stock, but only we
may mark it refunded, because that asserts money we paid.

## 12:35 — Replacement audit: the seller's exchange tick never reached the driver
Audited all five replacement/exchange phases against what is on disk. Everything held except one
seam. `collect_back` was translated into `task_leg='exchange'` only inside the `publish=True`
branch of `create_replacement_order` — the staff path. A seller raising a replacement gets a draft
with no task at all, so when staff later published it the task was born `single` and the tick was
silently discarded: no amber "collect the old item" banner in the driver's app, and the round trip
paid as an ordinary drop off the normal rate-card line.

Fixed at the root instead of the call site — the task-creation signal now derives the leg from
`order.collect_back`, so it is correct wherever the task is born. Proved the break first with a
failing probe (`collect_back=True, task_leg='single'`), then kept two regression tests: one that
the tick survives staff publication, one that an ordinary replacement stays `single`.

The existing `test_collect_back_is_honoured` only asserted the flag landed on the order row, which
is why this passed 107 green tests while being broken end to end.

## 13:12 — DRF 3.15.2 → 3.17.2 (upload-limit bypass)
The push surfaced a Dependabot alert, so I audited the installed environment rather than the
manifest: 11 packages carrying advisories, but only one reachable here. DRF 3.15.2's `request.data`
parsed the body without honouring DATA_UPLOAD_MAX_MEMORY_SIZE, which is an unauthenticated DoS
against 65 API views plus the public store API. The rest were code paths this app does not use —
the Django CVE is GeoDjango, wagtail is installed but not in INSTALLED_APPS, aiohttp is transitive
and the server-side smuggling needs it running as a server, and DRF's other advisory needs
AdminRenderer. deep-translator stays a known false positive.

Baselined the API suites at 150 green on 3.15.2 first, so an upgrade regression could be told apart
from anything pre-existing; still 150 green after. Proved the fix rather than trusting the version
number: the same probe raises RequestDataTooBig on 3.17.2 and silently parses a 50KB body against a
1KB limit on 3.15.2, which was run from an isolated --target install so production was never
downgraded. Kept that probe as a regression guard.

Also corrected two of today's entries that were stamped three hours late — the Django log lines
already render in Qatar time and I converted them a second time.
