
## 01:05 — One "Collect" figure for the driver, split on the way back
The driver app used to show two things on a P2P job: a "COD X QR" chip and a separate
"Collect delivery fee at the door" block with its own Mark-collected button. Per ops, the
driver should see ONE amount and never have to work out which half is the customer's
money and which is our fee.

`delivery/collect.py` is the new single answer: `amount_to_collect(task)` returns the one
figure plus its parts, and `split_collected(task, total)` splits what came back. The
client's COD is satisfied first, so an under-collection falls on our own fee rather than
on the business's money; an overpayment goes to COD, because it is the customer's.

The fee half lands in the new `DeliveryTask.fee_collected_amount` — deliberately NOT inside
`cod_collected_amount`, which is read in ~40 places and settles onward to the business; a
fee hidden in there would pay our revenue away. `live_cod_in_hand` counts both halves, so
the driver hands the whole pile back on the existing COD submission — one figure out, one
hand-in back. A new `fee_collection` transaction type keeps the two apart on the ledger,
and `billable_tasks()` now excludes a task whose fee was taken in cash, so the client is
never invoiced for money the customer already paid.

The split happens at the top of `driver_update_task_status`, before the 110%-of-expected
COD inflation cap reads the figure — a fee folded into it would have tripped the cap and
been clamped away. The external client webhook is deliberately left unsplit: a third-party
DMS reports COD and nothing else.

Verified end to end: Collect 340 (300 COD + 40 fee) → full payment splits 300/40; short by
20 leaves COD intact and takes 20 off our fee; overpaid 10 goes to COD; in-hand reads 340;
the task is not also invoiced. A normal delivery is unchanged (Collect 300 → 300/0).

## 02:20 — The hand-in screen did not agree with the balance
Follow-up to the Collect-figure work: `live_cod_in_hand` counted the cash delivery fee but
the driver's COD submission screen still summed `cod_collected_amount` alone. It would have
asked a driver for 300 while the system said he was carrying 340, and a fee on a job with
no COD at all was unreachable — that task never entered the submission queryset, so the
money would have stayed with the driver forever.

`WalletService.task_cash_leg()` is now the one per-task hand-in figure and carries the fee,
including on a task whose COD went through Fawran or a card, since a door fee is always
cash. The submission base queryset and the driver's manifest both widened to
`cod_collected=True OR fee_collected_amount > 0`. Verified that the balance, the screen
total and the sum of the per-task legs all agree at 465.00 across three shapes of task
(COD+fee, fee-only, plain COD).

Also fixed the staff Fleet Transactions ledger, where a `fee_collection` row was going to
render as an orphan: it fell through to the wallet-balance branch instead of the cash-in-hand
split and had no Settled/Pending state. It now reads like a COD collection in both columns,
with its own teal badge so staff can see which money is ours and which is the client's.

## 03:40 — Returned delivery: driver takes the fee at the door
Per ops, on a returned delivery the customer pays for the trip that was made and the driver
takes that cash at the door. Until now the fee went onto the client's invoice instead, and
nothing could ever mark it as collected in cash.

The Returning sheet in the driver app now asks for one "Amount Collected (QR)", prefilled
with that job's own charge (carried to the sheet as `data-charge`) and hidden entirely on an
unpriced job rather than showing a 0.00 box to reason about. On submit it posts
`fee_collected`, and `_record_door_fee()` books a `fee_collection` transaction, stamps
`fee_collected_amount` and re-syncs the driver's in-hand. It is idempotent on the task's own
field so a retried submit cannot book the money twice, and it never raises — a bookkeeping
failure must not undo the driver's status change. It runs BEFORE `open_return_for_task` so a
failure in the RMA cannot lose money the driver is already holding.

Because `billable_tasks()` already drops a task once `fee_collected_amount` is set, the
client stops being invoiced for it automatically — the fee is recovered once, never twice.

Also fixed: the Fleet Transactions COD tab filtered on
`['cod_collection','cod_driver_settle','cod_deposit']`, so a fee collection appeared on no
COD screen at all despite the row template being ready for it.

Order details now show the two halves separately — COD Collected, Delivery Fee Collected and
a Collected by Driver total — because the driver is shown one figure and never splits it
himself, so this is the only place the split is legible. The returned-parcel alert names the
fee too, with "(not billed to the client)".

Verified: a 35.00 door fee on a returned task books a `fee_collection` txn, moves in-hand
0 → 35, gives a per-task cash leg of 35, drops off the invoice queue, and a retry books
nothing.

## 21:57 — WhatsApp button now opens a compose popup

Turned the WhatsApp button on the driver verification queue into a popup instead of a
straight wa.me jump. Staff get the driver's name and number in the header, four quick
messages (greeting, documents unclear, vehicle details, ask to call), and an editable
body; "Open WhatsApp" hands the finished text to wa.me in a new tab.

Reused the page's existing modal machinery (openModal/closeModal, .uvl__modal__overlay)
rather than introducing a second pattern. The popup is worded to make the distinction
explicit: the green "Send reminder" modal POSTs to the backend and is logged on the
driver's record, this one sends from the staff member's own number and leaves no trail.

Two things cost time and are worth remembering. The staff session cookie is
`ezzy_sessionid`, not `sessionid` (settings.py:212) — a browser probe using the default
name silently lands on the login page. And the WhatsApp button sits inside a collapsed
`<details class="dvm__row">`, so a Playwright click needs the case opened first; a
force-click on the zero-size element does nothing and reads as "the handler is broken".
